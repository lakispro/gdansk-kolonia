### 1.2 When laid out / built up
* **Bärenweg**: a village road existing by the 19th c. on the north edge of Neuschottland, running from the old crossroads (today pl. Komorowskiego / Max-Halbe-Platz) east towards Schellmühl. Houses at its west end from the 1890s (first counted to Brunshofer Weg, from c.1900 to Neuschottland); the gardener Paul Lenz's nursery (Gärtnerei Lenz) at the start until c.1919; "Bärenweg" name from 1902. From 1907 the Reichskolonie was built at its east end, at the crossing with Posadowskyweg. "In the 1910s 11 houses were built on the street." House numbering was reorganised in the mid-1920s (today's numbers) — so **1920 numbers differ from today's** (see 1.3). Post office no.5 (1929, Adolf Bielefeldt), tenement no.3 (after 1920), Großsiedlung blocks on the north side (1920s; block Mickiewicza 8–14 completed March 1930 for Reinhold Brambach), tram line 6 IX 1930, Catholic Kreuzkapelle 1930 (Mickiewicza 24). Source: Gedanopedia MICKIEWICZA, ulica; https://jarekwasielewski.pl/zwrzeszcza/2021/05/blok-mieszkalny-z-1930-przy-ul-mickiewicza/ ; Gedanopedia TRAMWAJE.
* **Posadowskyweg, Marineweg, Neptunweg, Justweg**: laid out from 1907 (land bought 19 XI 1906; incorporated into Danzig 11 XI 1906), first houses 1907, "in the first place the section of today's Kochanowskiego and the area of the Klonowicza/Mickiewicza crossing were built", extension until 1915 "as far as today's ul. Modrzewskiego" (Justweg). The 1908-surveyed Meßtischblatt 1677 (printed 1910) already shows a block of buildings labelled "Kolonie" west of the railway. Sources: Gedanopedia KOLONIA; trojmiasto.pl Daniluk 2010; pl.wikipedia Kolonia (Gdańsk) https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolonia_(Gda%C5%84sk) ; Meßtischblatt 1677 Danzig 1910/1918 (mapywig).
* **Leegstrieß (Reja), Simsonweg (Dzielna)**: not yet existing in 1920 (see table); the ground between the Reichskolonie and Ostseestraße was "Baustellen" (Posadowskyweg 2/6 and 8/60 = "Baustellen" in the Adreßbuch 1920), plus from 1915 the first allotment gardens (Schrebergärten) at Traugutta and Kochanowskiego (ilangfuhr.pl "Kolonijki w Dolnym Wrzeszczu" https://ilangfuhr.pl/kolonijki-w-dolnym-wrzeszczu,48 ) and, from 1916, the football pitch "Reichskolonie-Sportplatz / Posadowsky-Sportplatz" (see 3).

## 2. The "Kolonia" neighbourhood: what it is and how it looked in 1920

### 2.1 Why "Kolonia"
The name is the Polish survival of **Reichskolonie** ("Reich Colony") — the official name (also *Kolonie Neuschottland* and *Neu-Schellmühl* before 1920) of a **workers' housing colony built by the German Reich (Reichsmarineamt = Imperial Naval Office) for the workers of the Kaiserliche Werft** (Imperial Shipyard, from 13 XI 1919 "Danziger Werft"). It has nothing to do with the Abegg Foundation (whose colonies are at Grunwaldzka/Bracka/Własna Strzecha and Chrobrego, 1895–1907), nor with allotment colonies (Kolonia Uroda = Laubenkolonie Frühlingslust 1928, Kolonia Zeńce = Erntedank 1923, Kolonia Zieleniec = Ostseeperle 1932, all north of Kościuszki), though Poles later used "kolonijki" for those allotments. Sources: Gedanopedia KOLONIA (J. Wasielewski, J. Daniluk); pl.wikipedia Kolonia (Gdańsk); Gedanopedia FUNDACJA ABEGGA https://gdansk.gedanopedia.pl/gdansk/?title=FUNDACJA_ABEGGA ; ilangfuhr.pl Kolonijki; Gedanopedia KOLONIE https://gdansk.gedanopedia.pl/gdansk/?title=KOLONIE .

### 2.2 Chronology
* 19 XI 1906: the Reichsmarineamt buys from the Schellmühl (Młyniska) estate 15 parcels, 64.53 ha, with 88 inhabitants, west of the Danzig–Neufahrwasser railway; the land is incorporated into Danzig (11 XI 1906 per Wikipedia; Gedanopedia MŁYNISKA: "19 XI 1906 the western part of Schellmühl (15 parcels, 64.53 ha, 88 inhabitants) incorporated into Danzig … a colony (Reichskolonie) was built there"). The land was "unbuilt, largely covered with orchards" (trojmiasto.pl Daniluk). The 1908 survey (Meßtischblatt 1677, ed. 1910) shows around it garden/orchard parcels (dotted "Gärten" signature), the Schellmühl estate farm to the east, the Strießbach to the south.
* 1907: first houses (Posadowskyweg and the Marineweg/Bärenweg corner first). Designs by architects of the **Technische Hochschule Danzig** (Gedanopedia) — and specifically by **Paul Kadereit (1877–1958)**, who "came to Danzig in 1907 as part of the great building project of the Wohnungsbaugesellschaft Neuschottland" and "designed the entire residential development along the newly laid-out Posadowskyweg" (de.wikipedia Paul Kadereit https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kadereit ; published in *Moderne Bauformen* 9/1910 p. 448: "Paul Kadereit, Danzig-Langfuhr – Arbeiter- und Beamtenhäuser der Wohnungsgenossenschaft Neuschottland in Danzig", https://archive.org/details/moderne-bauformen_1910_9 , scans in `img/moderne_bauformen_1910/`).
* Managed by the **Wohnungsgenossenschaft Neuschottland e.G.m.b.H.** (housing co-operative, office Fuchswall 6 in Danzig), founder and first manager **Maximilian Block (1853–1931)**. Planned for **1200 workers and lower officials** of the Kaiserliche Werft; flats "several rooms, sometimes with small gardens", "2 rooms, bright kitchen and bathroom" (fritzek blog) — a social showpiece "described in tourist guides" (Daniluk).
* 1 X 1890 railway halt "Schellmühl" opened on the Danzig–Neufahrwasser line; renamed **Danzig Reichskolonie** 1914; new halt building and platform 1915 (Gedanopedia PRZYSTANEK KOLEJOWY GDAŃSK KOLONIA https://gdansk.gedanopedia.pl/gdansk/?title=PRZYSTANEK_KOLEJOWY_GDA%C5%83SK_KOLONIA ). Trains from Danzig ran Neuschottland – Reichskolonie – Saspe – Brösen – Neufahrwasser.
* 1912: Provinzial-Hebammen-Lehranstalt und Frauenklinik ("Storchenhaus"/Bocianie Gniazdo) opened 17 X 1912 at Schellmühler Weg 1a (Gedanopedia SZPITAL POŁOŻNICTWA I CHORÓB KOBIECYCH).
* 1913–1916: **Christuskirche** (Protestant) on the south side of Bärenweg, architect Hermann Phleps, foundation stone 4 VI 1913, consecrated 31 VII 1916 in the presence of Crown Prince Wilhelm and Crown Princess Cecilie (pl.wikipedia Kościół św. Andrzeja Boboli; Gedanopedia KOŚCIÓŁ ŚW. ANDRZEJA BOBOLI; pastor 1916–1931 Alfred Semrau).
* 1915: colony extended to Justweg (Modrzewskiego); first Schrebergärten at Traugutta/Kochanowskiego.
* 1916: **Reichskolonie-Sportplatz / Posadowsky-Sportplatz** football ground at Posadowskyweg, home of **BuEV Danzig** (Ballspiel- und Eislauf-Verein) from 1916 — one of Danzig's earliest football grounds, on the site of today's GUMed pharmacy faculty pitch, oriented along the Strießbach (trojmiasto.pl "Początki futbolu…" ; en.wikipedia BuEV Danzig; Gedanopedia KOLONIA).
* 1907–1915 is thus the whole construction period; **in 1920 nothing new was being built** (Danzig had 8,260 unemployed on 3 I 1920; the city voted 20 million Mark for housing only on 30 VI 1920). The next wave came in the 1920s–30s: Ostseestraße extended to Bärenweg (after 1920), Großsiedlung Langfuhr (Bielefeldt/Kloeppel, from 1923/1925 north-west), block Mickiewicza 8–14 (1930), Helene-Lange-Schule (1929, on the football pitch), Kreuzkapelle (1930), tram (1930), the twin-house estate along Klonowicza north of Kościuszki, allotment colonies (1923–32).
* 1945: no major damage. 1951–55 Osiedle Roosevelta (Hallera, Ostroroga, Manifestu Połanieckiego, Racławicka, Klonowicza) built north and west of the colony; 1965 Mickiewicza 1/3; 1985 Mickiewicza 18; 2004–05 Mickiewicza 9. The halt closed 2005; allotments east of the railway cleared 2010 for the Euro 2012 stadium.

### 2.3 What stood where in 1920 (within ~150–500 m of Mickiewicza 43)
Reconstructed from the Meßtischblatt 1677 "Danzig" (survey 1908, editions 1910 and 1918), the Pharus plan 1941, the Adreßbuch 1920 and the sources above:
* **The colony itself**: a compact group of 2-storey (some 3-storey) houses in rows and pairs on Posadowskyweg (nos. 61–113), Marineweg (1–38), Neptunweg (1–15), Justweg and the east end of Bärenweg (4/5, 7–10f, 49) — "characteristic low and compact building" (Gedanopedia). Small front gardens with wooden picket fences, unpaved sandy roadway with young street trees and gas lanterns (1910 photographs). The square at the Bärenweg/Marineweg/Posadowskyweg junction was an empty "Marktplatz" (from the early 1930s a playground).
* **Empty plots and gardens**: between the colony and the Frauenklinik/Schellmühler Weg (Posadowskyweg 2–60 = "Baustellen") — fields, allotment gardens (from 1915) and the 1916 football pitch; **the Strießbach (Striessbach) brook crossed Posadowskyweg just south of house no. 61**, i.e. ~300 m south of Mickiewicza 43, and flowed east under the railway to Schellmühl and the Vistula.
* **Railway**: the Danzig–Neufahrwasser line (opened 1867) on an embankment ("Eisenbahndamm") ~150 m east/north-east, with the **Haltepunkt Danzig-Reichskolonie** (1915 building) at the end of Posadowskyweg/Justweg, ~400 m NE; Bärenweg crossed the line at a level crossing with a Bahnwärter house (Bärenweg 6, "Eisenbahnfiskus, Schulist, Bahnwärter"). East of the line: the Schellmühl estate, orchards, the harbour goods station Saspe (1913–17) and the port industries.
* **West/north-west**: open meadows and the remaining Schellmühl/Neuschottland orchards; the Ostseestraße did not yet reach the Bärenweg; beyond, the Großer Exerzierplatz and, from 1913, the **Langfuhr airfield** (Zaspa, ~1.5 km NW) and the Telegraphen-Kaserne (1912–16, Brösener Weg/Heeresanger, ~1.2 km NW; after 1920 civilian use — the Free City was demilitarised).
* **South-west**: the Christuskirche (1916) ~440 m W on Bärenweg; the older village-suburb Neuschottland (ul. Wyspiańskiego) with the Neuschottland school (1907), pub Grabbel, flour shop, the Abtsmühle pond on the Strießbach; the crossroads Max-Halbe-Platz (pl. Komorowskiego) ~700 m W.
* **Present-day buildings — which are from before 1920, interwar, post-1945?** (from OSM/Overpass building data, Gedanopedia, fotopolska and the 1920 numbering):
  * Pre-1920 (Reichskolonie 1907–15): Kochanowskiego 61–96 (2-storey, gabled), Klonowicza 3, 3A, 3B, 4–9 (2–3 storey), Mickiewicza 45 (2-storey) and 47/49 (3-storey) **[UNCERTAIN for 47/49]**, Sochaczewska old houses, Modrzewskiego colony houses; Christuskirche = Mickiewicza 11 (1913–16); Frauenklinik Kliniczna 1a (1910–12); Kolonia halt (1915).
  * Interwar: Mickiewicza 3 (after 1920), 5 (post office 1929), 8–14 (1930), 32–38 (Großsiedlung 1920s; OSM gives Mickiewicza 36 start_date 1920), 24 (Kreuzkapelle 1930); Hallera 107 (Helene-Lange-Schule 1929); the 3–4-storey blocks Mickiewicza 23–43 on the south side between Hallera and Kochanowskiego **[UNCERTAIN: most probably interwar (late 1920s–30s) infill of the former "Baustellen"; not documented in the sources found; the GEZ heritage register PDF (2018) lists no Mickiewicza/Kochanowskiego entries]** — so "Mickiewicza 43" as a 4-storey block may itself be interwar; in 1920 the player's house should be modelled as one of the 2-storey colony houses at the Bärenweg/Posadowskyweg corner or on Posadowskyweg.
  * Post-1945: Mickiewicza 26–30 and Klonowicza 2A–2F etc. (Osiedle Roosevelta 1951–55), Mickiewicza 1/3 (1965), 18 (1985), 9 (2004–05), the 1960s–70s blocks near Kościuszki, the Przedszkole nr 33 (the 1937–38 NSDAP house) at Klonowicza 1A.

## 4. Daily life of a Danzig shipyard worker in 1920

### 4.1 Political/economic frame (all from the *Danziger Jahreschronik 1920* transcribed on forum.danzig.de https://www.danzig.de/home/forum/stadt-danzig-mit-vororten-und-alles-was-danzig-betrifft/danzig-stadt/9528-danziger-jahreschroniken-1904-1934/page3 , Gedanopedia WOLNE MIASTO GDAŃSK 1920–1939, pl.wikipedia Wolne Miasto Gdańsk, en.wikipedia Danziger Werft https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danziger_Werft )
* 9/10 I 1920: Danzig separated from the German Reich under the Versailles treaty; 17 I–7 II German troops leave West Prussia; 24 I farewell of the Danzig regiments, 26 I the 128th infantry marches out, 7 II the 2nd Leibhusaren; 28 I British troops (Royal Fusiliers) arrive, General Richard Haking commands the Allied garrison; 11 II Sir Reginald Tower arrives as League High Commissioner. Allied troops leave 23–28 XI 1920.
* The **Kaiserliche Werft** (c. 7,000 workers at the end of the war; Danzig's biggest employer with Schichau) is closed as a naval yard; from 13 XI 1919 it continues as **"Danziger Werft"** under General Director Prof. Ludwig Noé, given by the German government to the city (Oct 1919); 1920 an early order from Poland for four river monitors; 1922 turned into the "International Shipbuilding and Engineering Company" (30 % French, 30 % British, 20 % Danzig bank, 20 % Polish Bank Handlowy); "until the end of the 1920s working well below capacity". Chronicle entries: 7–10 I 1920 strike of the employees on the Staatswerft; 28 II wage movement at Schichau; 30 II [sic] riots at the Staatswerft against Prof. Noé, the Magistrat threatens closure; **10 VI 1920 the Danziger Werft workers down tools over a wage dispute** (Danziger Volksstimme 26 VII 1920, No. 172), followed by the ship's carpenters at Klawitter; **16 VI–16 VIII lock-out at the Klawitter yard**; the Magistrat locks out 1,700 workers; 24–29 VI strike in the harbour; 29–30 IX strike in the Danzig state enterprises (Werft, artillery workshop, rifle factory — settled with 80–94 % of the demands, Volksstimme 29/30 IX 1920); **1 X 1920 great fire at the Werft, damage 2 million Mark**; 11 X trial run of the former SMS "Gefion" converted at the yard into the merchant steamer "Adolf Sommerfeld". 3 I 1920: 8,260 unemployed (6,817 men); 7 VI 1,000 unemployed at the Oberbürgermeister; 13 IV 2 million M voted for job creation; 26 X 7 million M for unemployment relief.
* Summer 1920 (Polish–Soviet war): 5 VII 63 Bolshevik prisoners freed from Polish escorts by workers at the station; **22 VII dockers refuse to unload the Dutch steamer "Triton" with Polish munitions** (unloaded by British soldiers; 31 VIII the dockers agree to unload); 29 VII demonstration in front of the Landeshaus against taxes and prices, Oberbürgermeister Sahm manhandled, police shoot one demonstrator; 9 VIII the Allgemeiner Gewerkschaftsbund resolves to boycott goods for Poland; 20 VIII the Constituent Assembly declares neutrality in the Polish–Russian war (trojmiasto.pl "Czerwony Gdańsk przeciw białej Polsce" https://www.trojmiasto.pl/historia/Czerwony-Gdansk-przeciw-bialej-Polsce-Wstydliwa-historia-z-1920-r-n50465.html ; chronicle).
* 16 V 1920 election of the Verfassunggebende Versammlung (turnout 69.5 %); 14 VI first session; 11 VIII constitution adopted; **15 XI 1920 the Free City of Danzig constituted/proclaimed** in the Assembly (Colonel Strutt for Tower); 7 XII Assembly becomes the Volkstag, Senate elected, Senate President Heinrich Sahm; 15 XII first Volkstag session in the former Generalkommando; 31 XII "Danzig without newspapers because of the printers' strike". Poland runs the railways (from 1921), has its own post, and shares the Harbour Board.
* Population: Danzig city 191,952 (1920); Free City 350,636 (end 1920); ~95 % German-speaking by the 1923 census; Poles 9–13 % (27–30,000) of the Free City in the 1920s, concentrated in the "Polenhof" around Kościuszki (Wrzeszcz) later; first Polish-language mass in the Langfuhr Herz-Jesu church 10 VIII 1919; Gmina Polska founded 21 IV 1921; Gedania sports club 1922. Many colony surnames in the 1920 Adreßbuch are Polish/Kashubian (Kossowski, Potrafki, Wisniewski, Czaja, Prziwarra, Schablewski, Kuzniarek, Glombowski…) — typical of the German-speaking but ethnically mixed Danzig working class. (Gedanopedia LUDNOŚĆ; pl.wikipedia WMG; Gedanopedia GMINA POLSKA; strefahistorii.pl)

### 4.2 Money, wages, prices
* Currency: the **German Mark** (Papiermark) remained legal tender until the Danzig Gulden (20 X 1923; coins from 18 XII 1923, Bank von Danzig 1924). Danzig issued its own **zinc 10-Pfennig coins on 17 VI 1920** (struck at the Danzig rifle factory) and Notgeld (Gedanopedia PIENIĄDZ; chronicle).
* Wages **[estimates, no Danzig-specific tariff found]**: G. Bry (NBER, *Wages in Germany 1871–1945*, ch. "Wages during War, Inflation, and Dictatorship", https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c2509/c2509.pdf ) — "wage rates in marks were roughly 3 times their 1913 levels by the end of 1918; 5 times by the end of 1919; 10 times by the end of 1920; 20 times by the end of 1921". Pre-war hourly rates in metalworking (Magdeburg, July 1914): skilled 52.9 Pf, unskilled 42.5 Pf. Hence in 1920 a skilled Danzig shipyard fitter/riveter earned very roughly **3–6 Mark per hour, i.e. ~150–280 Mark for a 48-hour week**, an unskilled Helfer ~2.5–4.5 M/h; the eight-hour day and 48-hour week had been introduced in Nov 1918 on all German yards, piecework (Akkord) abolished and re-introduced in 1919 (D. J. Peters, *Deutsche Werften in der Zwischenkriegszeit*, DSA 28/2005 https://urn.dsm.museum/DSA/DSA28_2005_095134_Peters.pdf ). Benchmark from the *Danziger Volksstimme* 29 IX 1920: members of the Stadttheater orchestra earned "monatlich nur 700 bis 750 Mark"; a double-bass string cost 96 M. was-war-wann.de gives a (non-official) average worker's monthly wage 1920 = 132 M — clearly too low for Danzig metal trades; use the Bry-based range.
* Prices (Danzig, 1920, chronicle & Volksstimme): rye bread 1.00 M/kg (31 I), 1.40 (10 V), 1.60 (25 VI), 1.80 M/kg (18 X); wheat bread 1.30 → 1.80 → 3.00 M/kg (Oct); **bread ration cut to 350 g/day (6 II 1920)**; butter rationed 62.5 g (March 1920, dag-forum); margarine free of ration cards from 31 VIII; potatoes: maximum price cut from 60 to 35 Pf per pound (July) — whereupon the farmers stopped delivering ("Der Mangel an Kartoffeln ist noch immer nicht behoben", Volksstimme 23 VII 1920; in Elbing potatoes cost 80 Pf/lb); the Wirtschaftsamt bought early potatoes from Poland; **tram fares doubled on 20 IV 1920**; the *Danziger Volksstimme* cost 25 Pf a copy, 1.10 M per week delivered, 4.50 M a month (masthead, 15 XI 1920); cake-baking from bread grain punishable by up to 5 years' prison and 500,000 M fine (Nov 1920); municipal income-tax surcharge raised to 550 % (21 V 1920).
* Consumer strikes and shortages: coal shortage stops the Kleinbahn (Jan) and threatens electricity (Feb); "Massenversammlung in der Sporthalle gegen Ernährungsnot" (4 III); food agreement with Poland 13 IV; "Schaufenster-Woche" 28 XI–5 XII.

### 4.3 Work at the yard
* Trades in the colony per the Adreßbuch: Schlosser (fitter), Schmied (smith), Kesselschmied (boilermaker), **Nieter** (riveter: Gerstenberger and Marschalkowski on Posadowskyweg 111–112, Henkel on Marineweg 4, Schulz on Marineweg 10, Wilhelmi and Malz on Neuschottland), Schiffszimmerer (ship's carpenter: Lück, Trohl, Bader, Holz, Bilski, Dehn…), Schiffbauer, Kupferschmied, Former (moulder), Modelltischler (pattern-maker), Dreher/Metalldreher (turner), Fräser (miller), Schweißer (welder — one, Stomrock, Neptunweg 4), Heizer, Maschinenbauer, Werkführer/Werkmeister (foremen, many "Mar.-Werkführer" = ex-navy foremen), Werftwächter (yard watchman), Werftbeamter, Oberwerftbuchführer. Riveting gangs: Nieter (riveter), Gegenhalter (holder-up), Vorschläger (hammer-man) and a Nietenjunge (boy heating rivets) — the Siedlungsstraße entries list "Vorschläger" too.
* Work signalled by the Werft bell/siren from the tower at the Werftgasse office (Gedanopedia STOCZNIA KRÓLEWSKA, 1878 drawing). 8-hour day since Nov 1918; Sunday free; Saturday half-day **[general German practice, not Danzig-specific]**.
* The yard in 1920: repairs and conversions (e.g. "Gefion" → "Adolf Sommerfeld"), ship-breaking of old warships (U-2 scrapped 3 II 1920, battleships scrapped 1920–21), the 1916–19 U-boat basin on Holm unused; a Polish order for river monitors; dismissals "not because of strike but lack of work" (Volksstimme, July 1920: the city's welfare committee voted support for "the dismissed of the Danziger Werft").

### 4.4 Consumption: beer, spirits, tobacco, newspapers
* Beer: **Danziger Aktien-Bierbrauerei (DAB) Kleinhammer** in Kuźniczki (Langfuhr) — Danzig's dominant brewery (130,000 hl in 1913), which after the 23 XII 1918 fire was rebuilt 1919–20 and bought up/closed the smaller Danzig breweries 1919–20 and the Zoppot brewery in 1918; its specialities: Jopenbier (the famous thick spontaneously-fermented Danzig beer), "Caramel", "Uhrbock", lager/Pilsener, later the "Artus" brand (Gedanopedia BROWAR W KUŹNICZKACH; inyourpocket "Local beer in Gdańsk"; barclayperkins blog on Danziger Jopenbier). Locally: the **Bergschlösschen-Brauerei Zoppot** (Wanninger) had its bottle depot at Bärenweg 3 until c.1920. Beer delivered by horse-drawn drays (DAB still had 60 horses and 8 lorries in 1923). A pub in 1920 within reach: "Grabbel, Restaurateur", Neuschottland 2/3; "Häusler, Gastwirt" on Schellmühler Weg; pubs and Gastwirtschaften on the Hauptstraße; **no pub is listed inside the Reichskolonie in 1920** (a co-operative colony without taverns) **[inference from the Adreßbuch]**.
* Spirits: **Danziger Goldwasser** and other liqueurs of "Der Lachs" (Breitgasse, since 1598/1704) — a bourgeois drink; the workers' drink was **Machandel** (juniper schnapps, Stobbe's Machandel from Tiegenhof, "the Danziger national drink", drunk with a dried plum); Julius von Götzen liqueur factory in Langfuhr (Kliniczna 1c!) (Wasielewski blog; de.wikipedia Machandel).
* Tobacco: Danzig cigarette factories in 1920 — **"Stambul" Tabak- und Zigarettenfabrik J. Borg** (Lastadie 35, since 1911; brands "Borg OO"), M. A. Hasse Tabakfabrik (since 1824), and after 1920 many small makers (Xanty-Jaka "Grossmeister/Memphis/Lord", Yenidze "Salem Gold", Vistulla, Sarmatia, Orient, Kismet on Hauptstraße 91 in Langfuhr, Hellas, Garbaty, Dukat); cheap loose "Feinschnitt" for hand-rolling, pipe tobacco (Kanaster, "Schwarzer Krauser"), snuff (Kownoer) and chewing tobacco (Kautabak) were the workers' staples; a Tabakschneider (Schrödter) lived at Marineweg 36 (trojmiasto.pl "Papierosy, cygara, tabaka" https://www.trojmiasto.pl/historia/Papierosy-cygara-tabaka-historia-gdanskich-wyrobow-tytoniowych-n172458.html ; Adreßbuch).
* Newspapers: **Danziger Volksstimme** (SPD, from 3 I 1920, "Organ für die werktätige Bevölkerung der Freien Stadt Danzig", second-largest circulation; 25 Pf), **Danziger Neueste Nachrichten** (largest, cheap, liberal), Danziger Zeitung (bourgeois; "Wer möblierte Zimmer sucht, inseriere in der Danziger Zeitung" — ad in the 1920 Adreßbuch), Danziger Allgemeine Zeitung (conservative, 4–6,000), Danziger Volksblatt (Catholic Centre), Gazeta Gdańska (Polish). Digitised Volksstimme 1920: http://library.fes.de/danzig/pdf/1920/1920-268.pdf (15 XI 1920) and -269.pdf (16 XI 1920, lead article "Die Proklamierung der 'Freiheit'"); July issues 168–172, Sept 228–229 downloaded to `research/volksstimme/`.
* Entertainment: Stadttheater (Rudolf Schaper; 15 XI 1920 "Blaufuchs" comedy), Odeon- and Eden-Theater cinemas (ads 15 XI 1920), the operetta on Langgarten ("Die Csárdásfürstin"), Danziger Messe 18–25 II 1920, football (BuEV on the Reichskolonie ground; SC Preußen Danzig-Langfuhr), bathing at Brösen/Westerplatte, the Jäschkental forest; Sunday walks on the Hauptstraße.
* Clothing **[general period knowledge, no Danzig source]**: workers wore the flat peaked cap (Schirmmütze), collarless shirt, waistcoat, dark wool jacket, cord or moleskin trousers, heavy boots or clogs in the yard; leather apron and cap for riveters; women in long dark skirts, aprons and headscarves; boys in short trousers; Sunday best = a black suit and bowler or cap. Ex-servicemen still wore field-grey uniform parts (there were "Kriegsinvaliden" in the colony: Behrendt, Marineweg 8).
* Home life: colony flats with 2–3 rooms, kitchen, own toilet/bathroom (a luxury in 1910), small front garden, wooden fences, a cellar; coal/wood stoves (coal scarce in 1920); water and gas laid on; the co-operative (Genossenschaft) collected the rent; food from the dairy shop (Schwarz, Meierei, Posadowskyweg 78), the butcher Hohmann (Bärenweg 10f), the baker Alt (Bärenweg 49), a Wäscherolle (mangle) at Posadowskyweg 99, the Danzig Konsumverein, and the allotments/gardens; bread and butter by ration card (Brotmarken were among lost-property items in the police report, Volksstimme 16 XI 1920).

### 4.5 Fifteen concrete, sourced details for dialogue / the intro story
1. The colony has no name-plate saying "Kolonia": everybody calls it **"die Reichskolonie"** or simply "die Kolonie"; its halt bears the sign "Danzig-Reichskolonie" (1914), though the old "Schellmühl" board is still visible on a postcard.
2. The player's rent goes to the **Wohnungsgenossenschaft Neuschottland**, office Fuchswall 6 in Danzig; old Maximilian Block runs it; a neighbour, "Wollermann, Gewerkschaftsführer", lives at Posadowskyweg 76.
3. The street name honours "Staatsminister Graf Posadowsky" — the 1920 Adreßbuch prints this explanation under the street header.
4. A riveter earns maybe 5 Mark an hour in autumn 1920, but a kilo of rye bread costs 1.80 M and wheat bread 3 M; the bread ration is 350 g a day; butter came on cards at 62.5 g.
5. On **10 June 1920** "die Leute der Danziger Werft" struck over wages; Klawitter's ship's carpenters followed; the Magistrat locked out 1,700 men until mid-August.
6. On 22 July 1920 the dockers refused to unload the "Triton" with Polish munitions — British Tommies did it; on 29 July a crowd beat up Oberbürgermeister Sahm outside the Landeshaus and the police shot a man.
7. The trip to work: over the level crossing at the Bahnwärter's house (Bärenweg 6), or the workers' train from the Reichskolonie halt to Danzig; from 1920 trams cost twice as much (fares doubled 20 April).
8. On 17 June 1920 the first Danzig coins appeared: **zinc 10-Pfennig pieces** struck at the rifle factory.
9. 15 November 1920: "Konstituierung der Freien Stadt Danzig"; the Volksstimme headline the next day: "Die Proklamierung der 'Freiheit'" — the socialists were not celebrating.
10. The British garrison leaves on 26 November 1920; the Fusiliers had lived in the Wiebe barracks.
11. The Christuskirche on the Bärenweg is only four years old (1916); pastor Alfred Semrau; the Crown Princess gave the font. Catholics walk to Herz-Jesu in Langfuhr (first Polish sermon there August 1919).
12. Local colour: a merry-go-round owner ("Schwarz, Karussellbesitzer") lives at Neptunweg 2; an actor (Neuhaus) at Posadowskyweg 7; a Bernsteindreher (amber turner) at 79c; the Zoppot brewery's bottle store is at Bärenweg 3.
13. Football: the BuEV plays on the Reichskolonie-Sportplatz by the Strießbach since 1916.
14. The Stadttheater's musicians earn only 700–750 M a month — "and a double-bass string costs 96 Mark now" (Volksstimme, 29 IX 1920).
15. On 1 October 1920 a great fire at the Werft did 2 million Mark of damage; on 31 December 1920 the printers' strike left Danzig without newspapers.

**Short intro (draft):** *November 1920. The wind off the Dead Vistula carries the smell of coal smoke and tar from the Werft. In the Reichskolonie the sand of the Posadowskyweg is frozen hard; behind the picket fences the last cabbages stand in the front gardens of the two-storey Genossenschaft houses with their steep red roofs and half-timbered gables. Franz K., riveter, thirty-one, walks out past the Bahnwärter's hut where the Bärenweg crosses the Neufahrwasser line, a tin of Feinschnitt in his jacket, 180 Mark of last week's pay in his pocket and the bread card in his cap. ## 5. Architecture and facades

### 5.1 The Reichskolonie houses (1907–1915, Paul Kadereit / TH Danzig)
Primary visual sources: *Moderne Bauformen* 1910, p. 448 (two photographs, `img/moderne_bauformen_1910/mb1910_p448_kadereit_*.png`; the same photos on fotopolska.eu/1105032 and /1105033 = "Kochanowskiego 82-84-86" and "Kochanowskiego 99 downwards", both dated 1910); the c.1914 postcard of Posadowskyweg 83 (`img/postcard_posadowskyweg_83_c1914_wasielewski.jpg`); three multi-view postcards (`img/postcard_reichskolonie_1-3_wasielewski.jpg` with present-day comparisons); the fritzek 2008 detail photographs; fotomemoria.pl 1980s–90s photographs; Commons present-day photos of Kochanowskiego and Reja.
Description:
* **Type**: low, compact "Kleinhaus" rows and semi-detached pairs, **two full storeys plus attic**, sometimes three storeys at corners; four to eight flats per house; wide steep **tiled roofs** (red pantile/Biberschwanz; some mansards) with dormers; **large gables to the street** (triangular and half-timbered — Fachwerk with plaster panels — or straight-edged brick gables); some houses with a shallow polygonal **bay window (Wykusz)** with an ornamental surround.
* **Materials/colours**: brick base and window surrounds in **red-brown Klinker**, upper walls **light plaster (cream/ochre/white)** in panels between brick pilasters (visible on the 1910 photo of the long gabled block: dark brick strips framing light plaster fields), wooden **verandas/porches** with turned posts, wooden shutters on ground-floor windows, small-paned wooden casement windows (usually 2 casements with 3–4 panes each in the upper part), doors under round-arched brick portals with sculpted keystones.
* **Ornament**: Latin and German mottoes in the gables ("**Nec temere nec timide**" and "**Ohne Fleiß kein Preis**" on Kochanowskiego 76; "Ohne Fleiss – keine Preis" on a bay), reliefs of **fantastic fish/dragons ("pomuchle" = cod)** over entrance portals (Kochanowskiego 68 and others), grotesque masks in gables — Heimatstil/Reform-architecture of c.1908 (gdanskstrefa "Domy, domy" https://www.gdanskstrefa.com/domy-domy-przechadzka-pierwsza/ ; fritzek blog http://fritzek-neufahrwasser.blogspot.com/2008/12/reichskolonie-czii-troch-historii-i.html ).
* **Street scene 1910**: unpaved sandy roadway (no cobbles in the colony; the Hauptstraße was cobbled with tram rails), wooden **picket fences** and low hedges around front gardens, newly planted lime/chestnut saplings on stakes, **gas lanterns** on cast-iron posts, children playing in the road, a cyclist — see the 1910 top photo.
* Rear: small yards/gardens, wash-houses, later garages; the colony had no Hinterhöfe of the Berlin Mietskaserne type (the only "Mietskaserne"-like house is Bärenweg 1 with ~30 households).

### 5.2 Other building types in the 1920 surroundings
* **Bärenweg west end / Neuschottland**: 1890s–1900s 3–4-storey brick tenements with plastered fronts, shops on the ground floor, courtyards with sheds (Bärenweg 1, Neuschottland 22a–d) — red/yellow Danzig brick, segment-arched windows, cornices; wooden gates.
* **Christuskirche (1913–16)**: neo-baroque, single tower with copper cupola, plastered walls with brick/stone dressings, red tiled roof — the tallest landmark visible from the colony (photo c.1920 `img/gedanopedia/baerenweg_christuskirche_c1920_gedanopedia.jpg` shows the church, the unpaved Bärenweg and low houses with fences).
* **Frauenklinik (1912)**: large neo-baroque/Heimatstil hospital with the stork figure over the entrance; photo c.1915 in `img/gedanopedia/frauenklinik_schellmuehlerweg_c1915_gedanopedia.jpg`.
* **Railway halt (1915)**: small brick/plaster station building with a hipped roof, wooden platform shelter, covered stairs to the platform (fritzek photos P1010090/P1010111).
* Interwar contrast (not yet in 1920): Großsiedlung blocks (1920s, 3–4 storeys, flat-plastered Expressionist/New-Objectivity façades, loggias), the brick post office (1929), Helene-Lange-Schule (1929 modernist).

## 9. The 20 most important facts (for the game bible)
1. "Kolonia" = **Reichskolonie**, a workers' colony built 1907–1915 by the **Reichsmarineamt** for ~1,200 workers and lower officials of the **Kaiserliche Werft** (from 13 XI 1919 "Danziger Werft") on 64.5 ha bought 19 XI 1906 from the Schellmühl (Młyniska) estate; managed by the co-operative **Wohnungsgenossenschaft Neuschottland** (founder Maximilian Block, 1853–1931).
2. Street names in 1920: Mickiewicza = **Bärenweg** (since 1902), Kochanowskiego = **Posadowsky-Weg**, Klonowicza = **Marineweg**, Sochaczewska = **Neptunweg**, Modrzewskiego = **Justweg**, Kliniczna = **Schellmühler Weg**, Wyspiańskiego = **Neuschottland**, Hallera = **Ostseestraße** (not yet reaching Bärenweg). **Reja (Leegstrieß) and Dzielna (Simsonweg) did not exist in 1920.**
3. The colony's architect was **Paul Kadereit** (came to Danzig 1907 for the project; own office 1909); the houses were published in *Moderne Bauformen* 1910 as "Arbeiter- und Beamtenhäuser".
4. House type: 2-storey (some 3) rows/pairs, steep red tiled roofs, big street gables (half-timbered or brick), brick base and surrounds with light plaster panels, wooden porches, small-paned casements, mottoes ("Nec temere nec timide", "Ohne Fleiß kein Preis") and fish/dragon reliefs over doors; front gardens with wooden picket fences; sandy unpaved streets with saplings and gas lamps.
5. The Adreßbuch 1920 lists Posadowskyweg 61–115 as Genossenschaft houses (officials at 61–71, workers at 79–113), Bärenweg 4/5, 7–10f and 49, Neptunweg 1–15, Marineweg 1–38; Posadowskyweg 2–60 were still "Baustellen" and the Strießbach crossed the road just south of no. 61.
6. Today's Kochanowskiego 61–96 keep the 1910 numbering (82-84-86 = Posadowskyweg 82-84-86, photographed 1910); Bärenweg was renumbered in the mid-1920s.
7. Neighbours in 1920 (Adreßbuch): riveters Gerstenberger and Marschalkowski (Posadowskyweg 111–112), ship's carpenters Lück, Trohl, Bader, Holz, Bilski; Werftwächter Hinz (104); union leader Wollermann (76); dairy shop Schwarz (78); butcher Hohmann (Bärenweg 10f); baker Alt (Bärenweg 49); merry-go-round owner Schwarz (Neptunweg 2); Bahnwärter Schulist at the level crossing (Bärenweg 6); the Zoppot Bergschlösschen brewery depot at Bärenweg 3.
8. The railway halt **Danzig-Reichskolonie** (opened 1890 as "Schellmühl", renamed 1914, new building 1915) on the Danzig–Neufahrwasser line, ~400 m NE; trains Danzig – Neuschottland – Reichskolonie – Saspe – Brösen – Neufahrwasser.
9. **Christuskirche** (Protestant, 1913–16, Hermann Phleps, neo-baroque, one tower), 440 m west on Bärenweg; Catholics went to Herz-Jesu (1911) in Langfuhr, 1.2 km.
10. **Frauenklinik/Hebammenlehranstalt** ("Storchenhaus", 1912) at Schellmühler Weg 1a, 700 m S; the **Neuschottland district school** (1907) at Neuschottland 35, ~700 m SW; football ground **Reichskolonie-Sportplatz** (1916, BuEV Danzig) and first allotments (1915) 200–300 m S.
11. No tram in the colony until 1930; nearest lines: 9 Langfuhr–Brösen (1901, Brösener Weg, ~1 km W), 8 Danzig–Neufahrwasser via Schellmühl (~1.2 km SE), 1/2 on the Hauptstraße (~1.3 km SW); Langfuhr station 1.35 km W; the Werft ~3 km SE (40 min on foot).
12. Surroundings in 1920: orchards/garden plots of the former Schellmühl estate, meadows towards Saspe, the harbour goods station and port industries east of the railway, the Strießbach to the south, the airfield (1913) 1.5 km NW, the emptied Telegraphen- and Husaren-Kasernen (demilitarisation 1920).
13. 1920 timeline: 10 I separation from Germany; British/French garrison from 28 I (Haking, Tower); 16 V constituent election; **10 VI Werft wage strike**, 16 VI–16 VIII Klawitter lock-out; 22 VII "Triton" munitions boycott; 29 VII riot, Sahm beaten; 1 X Werft fire (2 M Mark); **15 XI Free City proclaimed**; 26 XI British leave; 7 XII Senate (Sahm); 31 XII printers' strike.
14. Money: German Mark; Danzig zinc 10-Pf coins from 17 VI 1920; Gulden only from Oct 1923.
15. Prices 1920: rye bread 1.00 → 1.80 M/kg, wheat bread up to 3 M/kg, bread ration 350 g/day, butter 62.5 g on cards, potatoes 35–60 Pf/lb and scarce, newspaper 25 Pf, tram fares doubled 20 IV.
16. Wages (estimate): skilled shipyard worker ≈3–6 M/hour, 150–280 M/week in late 1920 (wage rates ≈10× 1913 by end-1920 per Bry); 8-hour day/48-hour week since Nov 1918; theatre musicians 700–750 M/month.
17. Beer: Danziger Aktien-Bierbrauerei Kleinhammer (Kuźniczki, Langfuhr — Jopenbier, Bock, lager), Zoppot Bergschlösschen; spirits: Machandel (Stobbe) for workers, Goldwasser (Der Lachs) for the bourgeoisie; tobacco: Borg's "Stambul" cigarettes, Hasse, loose Feinschnitt and pipe tobacco, snuff.
18. Papers: Danziger Volksstimme (SPD, from 3 I 1920), Danziger Neueste Nachrichten (largest), Danziger Zeitung, Danziger Allgemeine Zeitung, Danziger Volksblatt, Gazeta Gdańska (Polish).
19. Population: Danzig 191,952 (1920); ~95 % German-speaking; Poles/Kashubians 9–13 % of the Free City; many colony families with Polish/Kashubian surnames; first Polish mass in Langfuhr 10 VIII 1919; Gmina Polska 1921.
20. Post-1920 changes to avoid in the 1920 scene: Ostseestraße–Bärenweg crossing (after 1920), Großsiedlung blocks (1920s), post office (1929), Helene-Lange-Schule (1929, replacing the football pitch), Kreuzkapelle (1930), tram on Bärenweg (6 IX 1930), Leegstrieß/Simsonweg streets (1930s), NSDAP house and Skagerrakstein (1937–39), Osiedle Roosevelta (1951–55).
